I am a sociologist studying why and how individuals engage in political activism and civic participation. My research focuses on the social-network, identity-based, and emotional mechanisms that bring people into civic and political life, with particular attention to social movements and gender politics in South Korea and the United States.

I work with both qualitative and quantitative methods, including in-depth interviews, focus group interviews, survey analysis, and computational text analysis.

Outside of research, I enjoy running, working out, and reading Korean novels.

Research

Research Areas

Emotion and Political Activism

I examine how emotional ties to marginalized groups motivate individuals to engage in political activism and civic participation, and how this emotional pathway operates differently across political and social contexts.

Individualized Participation and Movement Organizations

I study how the shift toward personalized, network-based forms of engagement reshapes social movement organizations, exploring both the individuals who bridge across movements and the organizational challenges that emerge from changing participation styles.

Gender Cleavage and Politics

I investigate how gender conflict shapes political dynamics in South Korea, from the identity formation of young women in protest movements to the spread of feminist and anti-feminist discourse in online spaces.

Status Anxiety and Educational Competition

I explore how middle-class families in South Korea's highly competitive educational environment interpret and renegotiate experiences of success and failure, treating these not as fixed outcomes but as ongoing, dynamic processes of goal adjustment, and what this reveals about status anxiety and social reproduction.
