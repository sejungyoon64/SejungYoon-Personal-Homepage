Sejung Yoon

I am a sociologist studying civic engagement and collective action. My research follows three interconnected lines of inquiry: how individuals first enter civic and political life through emotional and experiential processes; how participation becomes sustained and increasingly individualized through networked forms of engagement; and how political cleavages, particularly around gender, emerge and shape collective alignment. I combine qualitative interviews, survey analysis, and computational approaches to study these dynamics.

Outside of research, I enjoy running, working out, and reading Korean novels.

Research

Research Areas

1

Pathways into Participation

I study how individuals first become engaged in civic and political life, focusing on the emotional and experiential processes that shape entry into participation. My work examines how feelings toward marginalized groups, as well as formative experiences such as success, failure, and evaluation, influence how people develop and adjust their orientations toward action.

2

Networked and Individualized Participation

I examine how participation becomes sustained, interconnected, and increasingly individualized in contemporary societies. My research explores how network-based forms of engagement enable individuals to move across issues and organizations, while also generating new challenges for collective coordination and movement organizations.

3

Political Cleavages and Collective Alignment

I investigate how political divisions, particularly around gender, emerge and consolidate over time. Drawing on large-scale online community data, I use topic modeling and co-occurrence network analysis to trace how feminist and anti-feminist discourses developed from relatively separate conversations into a structured, mutually constitutive cleavage. This approach allows me to examine the conditions under which gender became a central axis of political polarization in South Korea.
